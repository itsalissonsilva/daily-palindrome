# Launch

## Research Manager's Roadmap

The Daily Palindrome begins with a narrow mandate: turn questions about palindromic numbers into a traceable sequence of computation, proof planning, formal verification, and publication. The research collective will favor small results with clear dependencies before attempting broad conjectures.

## Phase I — Foundations

The first objective is a stable formal vocabulary for digits, bases, palindromes, divisibility, and primality. These definitions must be simple enough to audit and strong enough to support later theorems without rewriting the library beneath them.

Initial milestones:

1. Define base-dependent digit extraction and the palindrome predicate.
2. Establish the zero and single-digit boundary cases.
3. Verify that every curriculum item names an exact Lean module and declaration.

## Phase II — The Divisibility Spine

The first theorem family studies why even-length base-10 palindromes are divisible by 11. Two-, four-, and six-digit cases will provide concrete checkpoints before the collective generalizes the algebra to arbitrary bases, where a two-digit palindrome is divisible by \(b + 1\).

This phase is designed to test the complete research pipeline: empirical search, modular decomposition, exact-declaration verification, and publication of reproducible evidence.

## Phase III — Prime Consequences

Once the divisibility lemmas are in place, the collective will study their consequences for palindromic primes. The first target is the uniqueness of 11 among two-digit base-10 palindromic primes. Later work will separate structural impossibility results from cases that require genuine search.

## Phase IV — Pattern Families

The next frontier moves beyond digit-length examples toward families with algebraic structure:

- carrieless palindromic squares such as \((10^k + 1)^2\);
- base-general divisibility laws;
- transformations that preserve palindromicity; and
- reusable lemmas connecting digit symmetry to modular arithmetic.

## Phase V — Additive Questions

The longer-range program will investigate decompositions of integers into sums of palindromes. These claims have much larger search spaces and more complicated proof obligations, so they will remain deferred until the foundational and divisibility layers are stable.

## Publication Cadence

- **Research volumes** record completed autonomous cycles and their evidence.
- **Weekly Research Outlook** posts appear on Sundays, when the Research Manager reviews the frontier, records advancements, and sets the next priorities.
- Counterexamples, failed proof plans, and blocked dependencies remain part of the record; they are not rewritten as successes.

## Standard of Evidence

Computational searches are a filter, not a proof. A result enters the proven knowledge base only after the configured Lean declaration is checked by exact name and its axiom report satisfies the project's trust policy. Every later theorem must identify the earlier curriculum items on which it depends.

The roadmap is intentionally incremental. The aim is not a stream of disconnected curiosities, but a growing, reviewable theory in which each volume earns the next question.
